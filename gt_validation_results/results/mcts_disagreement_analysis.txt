MCTS vs GT DISAGREEMENT ANALYSIS
==============================================================================
Source CSV : gt_validation_results/results/mcts_vs_gt_results.csv (520 entries, unchanged)
GT         : GameTheoryMultiplayerAgent(depth=2)   MCTS: MCTSAgent(1000 rollouts, seed=0, c=sqrt(2))
Agreement  : same token index. (GT picks the lowest index among equal values; MCTS returns the lowest
             index of each equivalent-piece class. Disagreements between identical pieces: 0.)
Not rerun  : GT, MCTS, tournaments, depth study. Nothing in GT / MCTS / spots was modified.

Evidence labels used below
  [DATA]       counted directly from the CSV + spot boards (move types via the repo's classify_move()).
  [DERIVED]    arithmetic with GT's evaluator weights (0.0025*progress + 0.20*finished - 0.05*base +
               0.01*safe, centred on the player mean) computed offline on the boards. GT itself was not rerun;
               this '1-ply evaluator' reproduces GT's depth-2 label in 490/520 entries (section 7), so it is a good proxy.
  [HYPOTHESIS] plausible cause that the stored artifacts cannot test. The CSV holds only the chosen token;
               MCTS visit counts / win rates were not saved (MCTSAgent.last_search_info was not written).

4. FOCUS CATEGORIES

4.1 capture_vs_home_finish: 35/40 disagreements (87.5%), the largest single source
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GT preference   : finish the piece. GT finishes in 40/40.
MCTS preference : capture. MCTS captures in 35/40 and finishes in 5/40.
[DATA] Agreement 12.5% vs 50.0% chance: the two agents hold opposite preferences.
[DATA] MCTS capture rate by how far the victim piece had advanced (progress counted as in the evaluator):
         captured piece progress <=20   n=16  MCTS captures 12
         21-40                          n=12  MCTS captures 11
         >40                            n=12  MCTS captures 12
[DATA] The 5 agreements are all 'both finish'; victim progress there = [3, 7, 7, 10, 33] (4 of 5 are very low-value captures), dice = [2, 3, 3, 3, 5].
[DERIVED] Both options advance the same number of squares, so progress cancels. Finish earns +0.20; a capture earns
          +0.05 (victim back to base) + 0.0025*victim_progress, at most 0.18 (victim progress 52).
          So finish beats capture at the evaluator for EVERY possible board (margin >= 0.02 unscaled).
          On these boards the centred 1-ply margin (capture minus finish) lies in [-0.132, -0.010], never positive.
[DATA] GT depth 3 changes 2/40 labels here (2 of them to capture); MCTS matches GT depth 3 on 7/40 (vs 5/40 for depth 2). Disagreements by player count: 2p 13/14, 3p 11/13, 4p 11/13 (not a multi-player artifact).
[DATA] Spot-design note: the finishing token is the lowest legal index in 40/40 spots,
       so an index-biased player 'agrees' with GT here without reasoning about the tradeoff.
Likely causes:
  1. [DERIVED] GT's finish-over-capture preference is fixed by the evaluator weights (0.20 vs <= 0.18), not discovered by search.
  2. [DATA] MCTS values the move by win probability under random continuation. It captures more often the more valuable the
     victim (12/16, 11/12, 12/12 by band), i.e. its choice is value-sensitive rather than random.
  3. [CONTEXT] In this engine a piece on the home path moves only on the exact roll that reaches home end, so declining a
     finish has a real cost; MCTS evidently does not find it decisive. This is a genuine modelling disagreement, which
     means the paper's statement that finishing is correct and capturing merely delays the opponent (Sec. 6.3) is a
     normative claim that an independent search agent does not support.

4.2 safe_vs_openexisting: 31/40 disagreements (77.5%)
-----------------------------------------------------
Board structure : dice = 6 in 40/40; exactly 1 piece on the board and 3 in base in 40/40. Real choice: bring a 2nd piece out vs move the lone piece.
GT preference   : bring a piece out. GT does so in 40/40.
MCTS preference : safe-square move preferred (the 'safe' alternative is the lone piece landing on a safe square). MCTS moves the existing piece in 31/40 and brings one out in 9/40.
[DATA] Agreement 22.5% vs 50.0% chance (p <0.001): BELOW chance, i.e. a systematic opposite preference.
[DERIVED] 1-ply evaluator: bring-out = +0.05 (leaves base) +0.0025 (1 square) +0.01 (start square is safe) = 0.0625;
          moving the lone piece 6 squares = 0.015 (+0.01 if it lands safe). The margin is fixed by the weights, independent of the board,
          which is why GT chooses bring-out in 40/40 at depth 1, 2 and 3 alike.
[DATA] MCTS moves the lone piece, by how far that piece has already travelled:
         lone-piece progress 0-17   n=15  MCTS moves it 12
         lone-piece progress 18-34  n=16  MCTS moves it 14
         lone-piece progress 35-52  n= 9  MCTS moves it  5
         late (35-52) vs earlier: 5/9 vs 26/31; Fisher exact p = 0.168
[DATA] Agreement by player count: 2p 5/14, 3p 3/13, 4p 1/13
[DATA] GT depth 3 changes 0/40 labels; MCTS matches depth 3 on 9/40. The GT label here is depth-stable.
[DATA] Spot-design note: GT's choice is the lowest legal index in 40/40 spots (base tokens come first).
Likely causes:
  1. [DERIVED] GT's rule 'bring out on a 6' is the -0.05*base term. It is a weight choice, so it is not independent evidence
     that development is the stronger strategy.
  2. [HYPOTHESIS] MCTS values are win probabilities under uniform-random continuation with ~500 rollouts per root candidate
     (SE of a win rate ~0.02 at p=0.5). Tempo effects of one move are probably of that order, so part of the MCTS preference
     may be noise. The consistent 77.5% / 70% skew toward moving the lone piece suggests a systematic component as well.
     Cannot be separated without visit counts or more rollouts.

4.3 extra_turn: 28/40 disagreements (70.0%)
-------------------------------------------
Board structure : dice = 6 in 40/40; exactly 1 piece on the board and 3 in base in 40/40. Real choice: bring a 2nd piece out vs move the lone piece.
GT preference   : bring a piece out. GT does so in 40/40.
MCTS preference : advance the existing piece. MCTS moves the existing piece in 28/40 and brings one out in 12/40.
[DATA] Agreement 30.0% vs 50.0% chance (p 0.017): BELOW chance, i.e. a systematic opposite preference.
[DERIVED] 1-ply evaluator: bring-out = +0.05 (leaves base) +0.0025 (1 square) +0.01 (start square is safe) = 0.0625;
          moving the lone piece 6 squares = 0.015 (+0.01 if it lands safe). The margin is fixed by the weights, independent of the board,
          which is why GT chooses bring-out in 40/40 at depth 1, 2 and 3 alike.
[DATA] MCTS moves the lone piece, by how far that piece has already travelled:
         lone-piece progress 0-17   n=15  MCTS moves it 12
         lone-piece progress 18-34  n=15  MCTS moves it 13
         lone-piece progress 35-52  n=10  MCTS moves it  3
         late (35-52) vs earlier: 3/10 vs 25/30; Fisher exact p = 0.003
[DATA] Agreement by player count: 2p 6/14, 3p 4/13, 4p 2/13
[DATA] GT depth 3 changes 0/40 labels; MCTS matches depth 3 on 12/40. The GT label here is depth-stable.
[DATA] Spot-design note: GT's choice is the lowest legal index in 40/40 spots (base tokens come first).
Likely causes: identical to 4.2 (same board structure, same weight-driven GT rule).
[DATA] MCTS brings a piece out in 7/10 spots where the lone piece is late in its lap (Fisher p = 0.003); the same direction
       appears in 4.2 (4/9) but is not significant (p = 0.168).
[HYPOTHESIS] A runner close to home has less left to gain from another 6, so MCTS spends the move on development there.
Together 4.2 and 4.3 are the 'develop your board' half of the GT strategy that the paper credits GT with; an independent
agent does not reproduce it (agreement 22.5% and 30.0%, below the 50% chance level).

4.4 blocked: 22/40 disagreements (55.0%)
----------------------------------------
Board structure : legal moves per spot = {1: 1, 2: 31, 3: 8}. 1 forced-move spot (agreement is trivial there).
[DERIVED] Among the 31 two-move spots, 27 have IDENTICAL 1-ply evaluator value: both options advance the same dice,
          and neither captures, lands safe, or leaves base. The 'blocked' piece is simply not a legal option; what remains is
          a choice between two equivalent plain advances.
[DATA] GT picks the lower-index legal move in 26/31 two-move spots (84%); MCTS in 14/31 (45%, i.e. chance).
[DATA] In the 14 two-move disagreements GT took the lower index 13 times.
[DATA] GT's own depth-2 vs depth-3 labels differ on 6/40 blocked spots (depth2 -> depth3 token: {(1, 2): 6}): the GT label is unstable here.
[DATA] Split of the 22 disagreements: 14 are two-move indifferent advances (above); 8 are 3-move spots where GT brings a piece out and
       MCTS does not (same weight-driven pattern as 4.2/4.3, GT picks bring_out in 8/8); the remaining 0 are other.
[DATA] Agreement 45.0% vs 47.9% chance (p 0.754): statistically at chance.
Likely causes:
  1. GT's label is mostly the tie-break convention (strict '>' keeps the lowest index) plus small opponent-reply effects.
  2. MCTS is near-random on near-indifferent choices. Neither agent supplies a meaningful reference label here.
  3. The category tests legality (block_rate, invalid rate), metrics that do not use GT. Including it in GT-alignment adds noise.

4.5 home_entry: 20/40 disagreements (50.0%)
-------------------------------------------
Board structure : every spot offers {('advance', 'home_path'): 40} (home-path move vs plain main-board advance).
GT preference   : advance on the main board; GT enters the home path in only 5/40.
MCTS preference : enter the home path in 25/40 (62.5%).
[DATA] All 20 disagreements are 'GT advance, MCTS home_path': 20/20.
[DERIVED] The evaluator counts progress as rel+1 on the main board but as 52+home_step on the home path (= rel, one less).
          Entering the home path therefore costs exactly one progress unit (0.0025) against a same-length advance. Measured on
          these boards, 1-ply (advance minus home-path), in progress units: {1.0: 40}.
          That one-unit gap is the only 1-ply difference, and the 1-ply argmax equals GT's label in 35/40 spots.
[DATA] Stored GT depth sweep (home_entry_depth_sweep.txt), share of spots where GT enters the home path: depth 1: 0/40, depth 2: 5/40, depth 3: 20/40, depth 4: 31/40, depth 5: 38/40.
       MCTS (25/40) sits between GT depth 3 and depth 4: GT's preference moves toward MCTS as search deepens.
[DATA] MCTS matches GT depth 3 on 23/40 vs 20/40 for depth 2 (GT d2 != d3 on 15/40).
[DATA] Agreement 50.0% vs 50.0% chance: at chance.
Likely causes:
  1. [DERIVED] Evaluator off-by-one at the main-board / home-path boundary penalises home entry by one progress unit; with depth 2
     this is enough to make GT prefer a plain advance. This is the best-supported single-cause explanation in this study.
  2. [DATA] GT's label is depth-unstable (15/40 change at depth 3; 95% home entry by depth 5). MCTS has no depth cutoff and
     prefers the home path in most spots.
  3. Consequence: the paper's reading of GT as not prioritising home entry (Table 10 GT home-entry rate 0.13) is an artifact, so
     LLM-GT alignment in this category should not be interpreted as a strategy signal.

capture_vs_openexisting: 12/40 disagreements (30.0%)
----------------------------------------------------
[DATA] All 12 are 'GT brings a piece out, MCTS captures' (dice = 6). They occur only with 3 or 4 players (3p 3, 4p 9); with 2 players agreement is 14/14.
[DERIVED] GT's multi-player evaluator is centred on the mean of all players, so a capture credits only 1/n of the victim's loss
          while own gains count (n-1)/n. Capture beats bring-out (own gain 0.0625 vs 0.015 for a 6-square capture move) only if
          victim_progress > (n-1)*19 - 20, i.e. any capture for n=2, > 18 for n=3, > 37 for n=4.
[DATA] 2p: victim progress where GT captures: [10, 11, 12, 13, 13, 19, 19, 19, 24, 32, 32, 44, 45, 51]
       2p: victim progress where GT brings out: []
[DATA] 3p: victim progress where GT captures: [21, 21, 23, 24, 26, 34, 42, 42, 49, 49]
       3p: victim progress where GT brings out: [12, 16, 16]
[DATA] 4p: victim progress where GT captures: [32, 42, 49]
       4p: victim progress where GT brings out: [5, 6, 8, 13, 16, 16, 23, 25, 29, 34]
       Observed split matches the derived thresholds (3p: out at 12-16, capture from 21; 4p: out at 5-34, capture mostly from 42), with one 4p exception.
       Mean victim progress in the 12 disagreements = 17.2 vs 25.4 over all 40 spots: low-value captures.
Cause: GT's player-count-dependent discounting of captures; MCTS captures in 39/40.
(Compatible with the weak 4-player tournament result in GT_validation_REPORT.md, but this analysis does not show a causal link.)

grudge: 6/80 disagreements (7.5%)

Common feature of these four plus capture_vs_openexisting: the disagreements are concentrated on LOW-value captures (victim barely
advanced) and occur only with 3-4 players. The direction differs by alternative:
  - capture, capture_vs_openexisting: GT declines the low-value capture for a bring-out/advance and MCTS captures. [DERIVED] GT's

capture-vs-safe/home conclusions rely on.

8. KEY OBSERVATIONS
==============================================================================
1. The 204 disagreements are five different phenomena, not one:
   (a) Opposite weight-driven preferences, agreement BELOW chance: capture_vs_home_finish, safe_vs_openexisting, extra_turn = 94 (46.1%).
   (b) Near-indifferent choices where agreement is AT chance and GT's label is mostly its lowest-index tie-break: blocked, overshoot = 42 (20.6%)
       (of the 22 blocked ones, 8 are GT bring-out spots with the same cause as (a), so the purely indifferent share is 34).
   (c) A GT evaluator discontinuity plus depth instability: home_entry = 20 (9.8%).
   (d) Low-value captures in 3-4 player games: capture, capture_vs_home, capture_vs_openexisting, capture_vs_safe, grudge = 33 (16.2%);
       none occur in 2-player games. GT's centred multi-player evaluator explains the capture / capture_vs_openexisting direction [DERIVED];
       MCTS's reluctance to take trivial captures is the likely cause on capture_vs_home / capture_vs_safe [HYPOTHESIS].
   (e) Safe-square weight: safe = 15 (7.4%).
   Check: 94 + 42 + 20 + 33 + 15 = 204.
2. MCTS corroborates GT where the paper's capture conclusions live: 87.5% capture, 82.5% capture_vs_home, 92.5% capture_vs_safe, 70%
   capture_vs_openexisting, 92.5% grudge, with 100% agreement in 2-player games.
3. MCTS does NOT corroborate GT's finish-over-capture or develop-over-advance preferences (12.5%, 22.5%, 30.0% agreement; chance 50%).
   These are the preferences behind the paper's finisher/builder story, and GT's versions of them are fixed by its evaluator weights.
4. The GT label in blocked, overshoot and home_entry (120 entries) is not a usable reference: indifferent, tie-break driven, or
   depth/off-by-one driven. MCTS agreement there equals chance (45.0%, 50.0%, 50.0%).
5. The agreement rise from chance (49%) to 61% overall is carried by the capture categories; elsewhere the two agents are at or below chance.

==============================================================================
10. IMPLICATIONS, LIMITS, AND FOLLOW-UPS (none of the follow-ups were run)
==============================================================================
Implications for the paper
  - Do not present either GT or MCTS as ground truth. Report alignment against both and highlight the 316 entries where they agree
    as a consensus subset (strongest in the capture categories).
  - Soften Sec. 6.3-6.4: 'finishing is correct, capturing merely delays' and 'develop and finish' are properties of GT's weights;
    MCTS contradicts both.
  - Treat blocked, overshoot and home_entry as legality / indifference checks, not GT-alignment categories.
  - Report alignment by player count (2p versus 3-4p) because GT's multi-player labels differ in kind, not just in degree.
Limits of this analysis
  - MCTS is a single seed with 1000 random rollouts (win probability under random continuation), itself a noisy and imperfect reference.
  - n = 40 per category; p-values use the category's mean merged-piece chance rate, which is approximate for mixed spots.
  - The 1-ply evaluator is an offline re-implementation of GT's cutoff function, used as a diagnostic; it reproduces 94% of GT labels but is not GT.
  - Causes marked [HYPOTHESIS] are untested.
